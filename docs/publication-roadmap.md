\# Publication Roadmap



\## Project Title



Secure Shared Care Records: Security and Privacy Implications of Cross-Provider Access to Complete Patient Medical Records



\## Roadmap Purpose



This roadmap explains how the research project will produce at least three journal articles. The articles are designed as connected but distinct outputs. Each paper answers a different part of the overall research problem and contributes to the wider goal of developing a secure, privacy-preserving and clinically useful framework for cross-provider patient-record access.



The overall research programme investigates how complete or near-complete patient medical records can be made available across GP surgeries, ambulance services, urgent care, emergency departments, walk-in clinics, secondary care providers, specialist providers and community care interfaces while preserving confidentiality, proportionality, auditability, accountability and patient trust.



\---



\## Article 1: Conceptual, Policy and Risk-Taxonomy Paper



\### Working Title



Complete but Controlled: Security and Privacy Implications of Cross-Provider Access to Patient Medical Records in Integrated Healthcare



\### Main Purpose



Article 1 establishes the foundation of the research programme. It examines the policy, governance, legal, security and privacy implications of wider patient-record accessibility across care providers.



\### Main Research Question



What security and privacy implications arise from enabling cross-provider access to complete patient medical records for direct care?



\### Method



The article will use structured policy and literature analysis, supported by care-context mapping, data-flow modelling and preliminary security/privacy risk analysis.



\### Main Contribution



The article will contribute:



1\. A care-setting access matrix.

2\. A Level 0 data-flow model.

3\. An initial shared-record risk register.

4\. A security and privacy risk taxonomy.

5\. A policy-grounded discussion of clinical availability versus privacy/security proportionality.

\## Article 2: Empirical Stakeholder Study



\### Working Title



Trust, Access and Accountability in Shared Patient Records: Perspectives from Clinicians, Information Governance Professionals and Patients



\### Main Purpose



Article 2 will investigate how different stakeholders understand the benefits, risks and acceptable conditions of shared patient-record access.



\### Main Research Question



How do clinicians, patients and information governance professionals perceive trust, access, accountability and privacy in cross-provider shared patient-record systems?



\### Method



The article may use semi-structured interviews, focus groups, surveys or a mixed-methods design.



\### Potential Participants



\* GPs

\* Paramedics

\* Emergency department clinicians

\* Nurses

\* Pharmacists

\* Specialist clinicians

\* Information governance professionals

\* Caldicott Guardians

\* Digital transformation leads

\* Patients and carers



\### Main Contribution



The article will contribute:



1\. A stakeholder-informed understanding of acceptable access.

2\. Evidence on patient trust and transparency expectations.

3\. Evidence on clinician views of access, urgency and accountability.

4\. A set of requirements for designing secure and privacy-preserving shared-record access.

\## Article 3: Framework and Toolkit Paper



\### Working Title



A Context-Aware Security and Privacy Risk Assessment Framework for Shared Patient Record Systems



\### Main Purpose



Article 3 will develop and validate a practical framework or toolkit for assessing security and privacy risks in shared patient-record access.



\### Main Research Question



How can healthcare organisations evaluate and control security and privacy risks in cross-provider patient-record access without undermining clinical usefulness?



\### Method



The article will use design science research, threat modelling, framework development and expert validation.



\### Main Contribution



The article will contribute:



1\. A context-aware security and privacy framework.

2\. A risk assessment model for shared-record access.

3\. A practical checklist or toolkit.

4\. Synthetic care scenarios for testing access-control decisions.

5\. Recommendations for auditability, emergency access, sensitive-record segmentation and patient transparency.

\## Relationship Between the Three Articles



Article 1 defines the problem, maps the risk landscape and produces the initial taxonomy.



Article 2 validates and deepens the problem through stakeholder evidence, focusing on trust, accountability, clinical need and acceptable access.



Article 3 translates the conceptual and empirical findings into a practical framework or toolkit for secure, privacy-preserving shared patient-record access.



Together, the three articles form a coherent research programme:



\*\*Problem mapping → stakeholder evidence → framework/toolkit development\*\*



\---



\## Immediate Publication Strategy



The first priority is Article 1 because it can be developed immediately from policy sources, academic literature, care-scenario mapping, data-flow modelling and risk analysis. It does not require ethics approval because it does not yet involve human participants.



Article 2 should begin after ethics preparation because it will require stakeholder engagement.



Article 3 should be developed after the risk taxonomy and stakeholder findings are mature enough to inform the framework design.
