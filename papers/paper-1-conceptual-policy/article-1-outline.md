# Article 1 Outline

## Working Title

Complete but Controlled: Security and Privacy Implications of Cross-Provider Access to Patient Medical Records in Integrated Healthcare

## Proposed Abstract Structure

### Background

Healthcare systems are moving toward integrated patient records to support safer, faster and more coordinated care across primary, urgent, emergency, secondary and specialist care.

### Problem

While fuller record access may improve care delivery, it also expands the security and privacy risk landscape by increasing the number of users, systems, roles, suppliers, devices and access contexts involved in patient data processing.

### Aim

This article analyses the security and privacy implications of cross-provider patient-record access and proposes an initial risk taxonomy for secure shared-care record governance.

### Method

The study uses a structured policy and literature analysis, supported by data-flow modelling and security/privacy threat modelling.

### Contribution

The article contributes a care-context access matrix, an initial data-flow model and a security/privacy risk taxonomy for shared patient-record access.

## Main Argument

Integrated patient records should not be evaluated only as interoperability infrastructure. They should be treated as socio-technical security and privacy systems whose safety depends on access justification, role clarity, auditability, patient transparency, proportionality, supplier assurance and clinical context.
